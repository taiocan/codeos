# Doctrine — v11

Doctrine v11 incorporates Doctrine v10 in full and amends D1, D2 and D11 for Charter obligations.

## Charter obligations

The approved Solution Charter owns, in addition to what D1 assigns it, solution-level
**Requirements** (`R-#`) and **Milestones** (`M-#`). Outcomes (`O-#`), Requirements, System
Constraints (`C-#`) and Milestones are Charter obligations. Each establishes a different claim:

| Obligation | Satisfied when |
|---|---|
| Outcome | the product delivers the measurable result, normally shown by integrated product-journey or evaluation evidence |
| Requirement | the specified product behavior is implemented and verified |
| System Constraint | the implementation respects the invariant or boundary |
| Milestone | its stated acceptance condition is achieved |

Every obligation has a stable identity that is never reused. An obligation is atomic when one
independently observable violation establishes nonconformance without relying on an unrelated
obligation. Conditions that form one invariant stay together. Charter review challenges compound
obligations before approval.

An obligation's meaning is identified separately from the Charter as a whole. A change to an
obligation's meaning invalidates its established coverage and verification even when its identity
is unchanged; history is kept but never establishes current compliance. The integrity of the
Charter as a whole artifact is preserved unchanged.

## Accountability and verification

Each obligation has exactly one accountable verification owner and one or more mandatory checks.
A contribution from another feature never establishes compliance. A milestone aggregates evidence
that the existing product-journey, integration, qualification and evaluation mechanisms produce.

Allocation, specification sufficiency and verification are separate claims and MUST be reported
separately. An obligation is verified only when every mandatory check has current passing evidence
bound to the current obligation meaning, implementation and configuration. Missing, failed,
skipped, stale or ambiguous evidence never establishes verification. An obligation that has not
been assessed under this doctrine is reported as unassessed, never as verified.

A semantic reviewer's judgment of whether a check would detect a violation remains advisory. An
acceptance claim requires verification of the obligations it claims.

Every other guarantee of v10 is unchanged.
