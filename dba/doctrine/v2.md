# DBA Doctrine — v2

DBA turns a human-approved specification into working, observable software. Detailed procedures,
prompts, templates, review rules, architecture mechanisms, file layouts, and tool instructions live
in their owning components rather than in this doctrine.

## Authority

The human's current explicit decision has highest authority over product meaning.

Intent, Contract, and Event Schema are distinct parts of one approved **Specification Package**.
Together they define required behavior. Applicable approved architecture defines project-level
structural commitments but MUST NOT invent or override behavior.

Runtime evidence and tests establish what the system currently does. They MUST NOT silently amend
approved requirements. Safety, authorization, and integrity requirements remain binding when
observed behavior violates them. Unresolved authority conflicts MUST be surfaced to the human.

## DBA Lifecycle

Intent, Contract, and Event Schema MAY be drafted and revised together. No approval is required
between them. Before implementation, they MUST be mutually consistent and receive one explicit
human approval as a Specification Package.

Package approval authorizes the bounded delivery cycle:

`Implementation → Tests → Runtime Evidence → Reconciliation → Replay`

The cycle has no intermediate human gate. Final acceptance remains a human decision. A human MAY
request an additional checkpoint at any time.

## Behavioral Constraints

Implementation MUST conform to the approved Specification Package and applicable architecture.
It MAY use normal internal structures, technical errors, validation, logging, and established
project patterns when they do not change approved behavior, authority, event semantics,
architecture, safety, authorization, or integrity.

Only Contract-approved behavioral failures may be exposed as governed failure outcomes. Only
Event-Schema-approved events may be emitted as governed events. Internal technical errors MAY be
richer, but MUST remain distinguishable from governed behavioral failures. Behavior MUST NOT be
hidden from the approved artifacts that govern it.

## Human Control and Implementation Freedom

One human decision approves the complete Specification Package. The three artifacts record that
same decision consistently; their status fields are distributed records, not separate gates. The
approval applies only while all three continue to represent the jointly approved specification.

Actions designated as controlled require human authorization. Package approval does not authorize
the agent to redefine the requested outcome, make an unresolved product or architecture decision,
or bypass an applicable safety, authorization, or integrity constraint.

## Escalation and Verification

Return to human governance when the work requires changing approved behavior, authority, event
semantics, project-level architecture, safety, authorization, or integrity. A repair that only
restores conformance with already-approved meaning does not require a new product decision, but it
MUST receive verification proportional to its risk.

Runtime evidence used for verification MUST NOT be fabricated or silently rewritten. Tests,
runtime evidence, reconciliation, and replay determine whether implementation conforms. A mismatch
MUST be reported and resolved explicitly; observed behavior never becomes approved merely because
it occurred.
