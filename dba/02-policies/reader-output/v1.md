# Reader Output Policy — v1

## Summary

- This policy owns the operational detail of doctrine **D10 — human reviewability**. It applies to
  every human-readable output produced by a Codeos-owned path under a configuration that selects
  it: governed and non-governed artifacts, generated reports, reviewer and workflow output, and
  substantial inline agent responses.
- Correct content that a human cannot efficiently absorb, distinguish, and review does not satisfy
  D10. Structure the output so the main result comes first, distinct domains are separated, and
  decision, evidence, gaps, and actions stay distinguishable.
- Mechanical checks verify only cheap structure. Whether prose is semantically clear is a human,
  agent, and reviewer judgment.

Oversimplification risk: this summary omits the specific rules below. Read the relevant section
before relying on it for a consequential decision.

This policy controls presentation only. The user's instruction, governed artifacts, and
artifact-specific syntax remain authoritative. Preserve technical meaning and logical precision
over stylistic variety. Canonical terminology applies even to a one-sentence response; the
progression rules apply when the output carries enough explanatory structure for them to be useful.

## Open With the Result

Lead with the main result, recommendation, or decision.

For a complex output with several important findings, begin with a short summary that previews the
important findings, then explain them in the same order. Omit a summary that would only repeat the
answer. A summary orients the reader; it never replaces the detail behind it.

## Semantic Separation

Separate distinct meaning rather than compressing it.

- **Section separation** — a section answers one identifiable reader question or serves one clear
  semantic purpose. Do not make one section carry an unrelated second question.
- **Paragraph separation** — a paragraph normally develops one stable topic, reasoning step,
  finding, decision, or purpose. Start a new paragraph when the primary logical subject changes.
- **Lean, not compressed** — brevity MUST NOT be achieved by mixing semantic domains when
  separating them materially lowers the reader's cognitive load.

## Status Separation

Within any output, these MUST remain distinguishable rather than blended into one dense statement:

decision · observation · reasoning · evidence · assumption · unresolved gap · next action.

A reader must be able to tell what was decided from what was merely observed, what is proven from
what is assumed, and what remains open from what is settled.

## Explicit Relationships

When a decision, its rationale, its evidence, its impact, and the resulting action must be
connected, state the relationships explicitly. Do not compress them into a single dense sentence or
paragraph that forces the reader to reconstruct which part is which.

## Progressive Understanding

Introduce new meaning from context the reader can already identify.

- **Known before new** — do not require several undefined concepts to be decoded at once. Begin
  each reasoning step from information already established, then introduce the next concept.
- **Sentence-to-sentence continuity** — within explanatory prose, connect each sentence to
  information identifiable from the preceding context before introducing new information. The
  requirement is a recognizable information connection, not an identical sentence opening; do not
  force grammatical repetition when it makes a sentence unnatural or less precise.
- **Canonical terminology** — consult `dba/05-guidance/terminology.md` for Codeos and DBA concepts
  and `.codeos/00-project/terminology.md`, when it exists, for project-domain concepts. Reuse a
  canonical term exactly and consistently. A project glossary must not silently redefine a Codeos
  or DBA term; surface the conflict instead of guessing. Use a standard term consistently for a
  missing ordinary engineering term and define it briefly when needed. For a missing recurring
  project-specific term, establish it through the terminology mechanism when authorized, otherwise
  present the proposed term and definition explicitly. Do not substitute synonyms for variety, and
  do not add stylistic vocabulary to a glossary.

## Choose a Reader-Oriented Progression

Use the simplest progression that fits the explanation, and make the structure actually follow it.
Do not name the pattern in the output unless naming it helps the reader. Patterns may be combined
when useful; do not combine them mechanically.

### Stable Topic

Several sentences with a recognizable shared topic, for the properties of one concept:

```text
Topic -> fact A
Topic -> fact B
Topic -> fact C
```

Do not change the subject when each sentence still describes the same thing.

### Known-to-New Progression

A deliberate chain when one newly introduced concept becomes the subject of the next reasoning
step — for workflows, causality, transformations, and logical derivations:

```text
A -> B
B -> C
C -> D
```

### Whole Before Parts

Name the whole, relationship, or category before its parts. Use this for architecture,
decomposition, responsibilities, and sibling concepts whose relationship matters.

### Preview Then Traverse

For several important findings, changes, risks, decisions, or recommendations, state the important
parts first and explain them in that same order.

## Navigable Detail

A reader MUST be able to move from summary → relevant section → supporting evidence without reading
everything sequentially. Summaries orient; they do not replace detail. Structure headings and
openings so the path from the high-level result to its evidence is discoverable.

## Metadata

A substantial narrative artifact declares
`reader_model: <stable-topic | known-to-new | whole-before-parts | preview-then-traverse>` in its
frontmatter and opens with a Summary block immediately after that frontmatter, in the shape the
Downstream Project Layout Contract defines. Both requirements are exempt on event schemas, logs,
machine-structured data, compact tables, and any artifact short enough that a summary would repeat
it.

## Inline Responses

A substantial inline agent response follows the same reader-oriented progression — result first,
semantic domains separated, decision, evidence, gaps, and actions kept distinct, no dense
multi-domain paragraphs — without requiring frontmatter or a visible `reader_model` declaration.

## Preserve Semantic and Formal Structure

Reader-oriented restructuring MUST NOT:

- change governed meaning, canonical terminology, exact identifiers, names, or literals;
- weaken a condition, merge distinct requirements, or remove a necessary qualification;
- introduce an unsupported conclusion or convert uncertainty into certainty;
- convert advisory language into mandatory language;
- invent missing technical facts.

Formal artifact rules take precedence over this policy. Do not mechanically transform source code,
schemas, event payloads, identifiers, literal values, commands, logs, generated machine-readable
data, compact tables, or formal syntax whose structure is already defined.

## Reviewer Expectations

The independent reviewer assesses whether a substantial reviewed output meets D10 and flags a
material semantic-readability failure — a buried result, a section carrying several unrelated
questions, decision and evidence and open gaps blended together, or a declared reader model the
structure does not follow. The reviewer records this as a finding for human judgment. Neither the
reviewer nor any mechanical check asserts that prose is semantically clear.

## Delivery Invariant

Under a configuration selecting this policy, every Codeos-owned producer of substantial
human-readable meaning is covered, in one of two ways:

- **Instruction-driven producers** — a normal agent, and any isolated provider such as the
  independent reviewer — MUST receive this policy and the applicable terminology in their effective
  input. A normal-agent instruction directs the agent to read and apply the Reader Output policy
  the adopted DBA configuration selects. An isolated provider receives the exact selected-policy
  and terminology bytes in its frozen input, resolved from that configuration and delivered inside
  the provider's own isolation and hash boundary.
- **Deterministic tool output** — `codeos-workflow`, the initializer, and similar tools whose text
  is fixed by code, not generated by a model — MUST implement and conform to the applicable
  structural consequences (result first, status and detail distinguishable, one concrete next
  action). It does not receive this policy as injected prose.

Exclusively machine-structured outputs are exempt. Terminology supplied to an instruction-driven
producer is language and interpretation context, never acceptance evidence or a substitute for
requirements. Integration tests demonstrate the applicable route for each covered producer.
