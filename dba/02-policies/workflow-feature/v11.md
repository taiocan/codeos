# Feature Development Workflow Contract — v11

v11 incorporates v10 and scopes production-source evidence to what it observed and to the verifier
that produced it. It also lets a claim inherit upstream production truth.

## Evidence Scopes

A project may declare named Evidence Scopes under `reality_verification` in `codeos.yaml`:

```yaml
reality_verification:
  command: scripts/verify-production-claims.mjs
  scopes:
    provider: [backend]
    web: [web]
```

Each scope is a non-empty list of existing, non-traversing, project-relative paths. Codeos passes
the declared scopes to the verifier. The verifier selects from them and never defines their
contents: for each reality-dependent claim, the evidence states in `claim_scopes` the declared
scope names that claim observed. These conditions fail the verification:
- an undeclared or duplicated scope name;
- a malformed declaration;
- an unresolved path;
- a `claim_scopes` entry for a claim the evidence does not cover.

A claim with no stated scope observed every implementation path.

## Production-source evidence

A `production_source_evidence` record binds:
- the Intent, Contract and Event Schema;
- `evidence_scope_state`, the content of every non-test file under the union of its claims'
  scope paths. For each scope path, this also covers the manifests and lockfile of the Cargo or npm
  package that contains it, and always the project's `docker-compose.yml`. When any claim observed
  every implementation path, the union is every implementation path;
- `reality_verifier`, the content identity of the configured verifier command file.

A change confined to test files therefore leaves the evidence current. Each of these makes it
stale:
- a change to an observed scope;
- a change to its package manifests or lockfile;
- a change to the runtime composition;
- a change to the verifier.

**The verifier binding is an evidence-regeneration dependency, not by itself a post-acceptance
validity dependency.** A verifier change makes recorded evidence stale wherever that evidence is
still needed before acceptance or during revalidation, and `check` regenerates it. It never by
itself makes an accepted feature's Current Validity stale.

A record made under an earlier version lacks these bindings and is stale under v11.

## F6 Reconciliation

A new F6 receipt binds the non-test product state in place of the implementation state. A
receipt recorded under an earlier version keeps the binding it recorded, and stays current while
that binding is unchanged.

## Inherited claims

The Reality Basis may classify a claim `inherited`. Its production-source cell names exactly one
upstream claim, as `F-NNNN/claim-id`, and that claim must be classified `reality-dependent` in the
upstream feature's current Contract. The claim's other source cells are completed as for a
reality-dependent claim. An inherited claim creates no observation, and needs no production run
of its own.

An inherited claim is current only while all of these hold:
1. the upstream feature's Current Validity is VALID;
2. the production evidence selected in its accepted Candidate Bundle covers the upstream claim.

Until then, F5 blocks. After acceptance, losing either condition makes the feature's Current
Validity stale until the upstream feature is valid again. The claim always follows the upstream's
current selected evidence and never an older proof. The Candidate Bundle records, for each
inherited claim, the upstream feature, claim, accepted bundle and selected evidence at candidate
time.

The feature's own fixture verification still covers how it renders the inherited value. Only
v11 recognizes the classification. Contracts approved earlier keep their claims until their next
semantic revision.

F4 and F8 Observed Surface semantics, Candidate Bundle input derivation, and the acceptance
currency of implementation and governed inputs are unchanged. Review readiness judges evidence
currency by these bindings.
